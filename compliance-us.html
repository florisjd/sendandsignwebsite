Compliance with the US ESIGN Act, UETA & New York ESRA.

For simple commercial contracts governed by US law, Send & Sign is engineered around the federal Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN, 15 U.S.C. §§ 7001–7031), state Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) laws, and New York's Electronic Signatures and Records Act (ESRA, N.Y. State Technology Law Art. 3).

Jurisdiction-aware signing

Two US governing-law choices.

Because New York has not adopted UETA, senders choose between two explicit US legal frameworks in Step 1 of the New Envelope wizard. Unknown governing-law codes are rejected by the signing service.

All US states except New York

Federal ESIGN Act & state UETA

Applies the server-owned US consumer disclosure (US-ESIGN-UETA-v3), records scope tag SimpleContract-US-ESIGN-UETA, labels the certificate with ESIGN & state UETA, and displays timestamps in UTC and US Eastern time (EST/EDT).

New York

Federal ESIGN Act & New York ESRA

Applies the US disclosure naming NY ESRA, records scope tag SimpleContract-US-ESIGN-NYESRA, prints the New York ESRA scope line and stamp label on the certificate and PDF, and displays timestamps in UTC and US Eastern time (EST/EDT).

ESIGN, UETA & NY ESRA

How Send & Sign meets each US requirement.

The US workflow uses the same tamper-evident evidence chain as Australian envelopes, adding full ESIGN § 7001(c) consumer disclosures, enforced pre-signing delivery proof and US Eastern time display.

Statutory or case-law requirement How Send & Sign meets it
Intent to sign
ESIGN § 7001(a), § 7006(5); UETA § 7, § 2(8); NY ESRA § 304(2)		 The signer draws, types or uploads a signature, ticks the consent box, and completes a deliberate Sign and complete action (or Decline with a reason). IntentConfirmed is recorded in the hash-chained audit trail.
Agreement & consumer disclosure
UETA § 5(b); ESIGN § 7001(c)(1)		 Before consenting, the signer sees the server-owned US-ESIGN-UETA-v3 disclosure verbatim (the portal fails closed if no wording is served): scope (this document and transaction only); the right to a paper copy before or after signing at no charge; how to withdraw by selecting Decline (at no cost, ending electronic signing and recording the decision for the sender); how to request paper or update contact details using the sender's email displayed alongside the notice; hardware and software requirements; and confirmation that they can open the document on their device. The version and SHA-256 hash of the exact wording are recorded per signer and printed on the certificate.
Reasonable demonstration of access
ESIGN § 7001(c)(1)(C)(ii)		 Enforced technically rather than assumed: the signing service records the first post-code delivery of the PDF or HTML document per recipient (documentDeliveredAt + a hash-chained Viewed event) and refuses a US signature (409) until delivery has occurred. The portal keeps consent and signing disabled until the document renders on the device, and the delivery timestamp is printed on the certificate.
Attribution & security procedure
UETA § 9; Ruiz v. Moss Bros.; Bannister; Espejo		 Unique 256-bit single-use token per recipient plus a mandatory 6-digit email verification code before the document, evidence pack or signing endpoint unlock (link-only signing is refused; five wrong codes lock the check). Signer identity is bound to the verified recipient record, with per-signer real IP address, browser and timestamps captured on the certificate.
Retention & accurate reproduction
ESIGN § 7001(d)–(e); UETA § 12		 Computes server-verified OriginalSha256 and SignedSha256, maintains an RFC 8785 (JCS) SHA-256 hash-chained audit trail, attaches the signed PDF (up to 10 MB) to completion emails for the sender and every signer, and saves the signed copy back into your SharePoint folder next to the untouched original.

Evidence on every US document

Certificate, delivery proof & privacy notice.

  • Multi-page Certificate of Electronic Signature: prints the readable governing-law line, sender, sent and completed times, identity check used, and for every signer: OTP verification time, document delivery time, signing time, IP address, browser, adopted name, consent version + text SHA-256, and every audit event.
  • Consistent US Eastern display time: certificates and audit trails show UTC and US Eastern time (EST/EDT); visual signature stamps and email expiry notices use EST/EDT.
  • US privacy notice: US-law envelopes display a Privacy & ESIGN Record Notice in the signing portal instead of the Australian APP 5 notice.
  • Exportable evidence pack: download the RFC 8785 hash-chained JSON evidence pack for independent verification during audits or disputes.

Stated plainly

Current review status & roadmap

We state clearly what is built today and what remains before general US commercial use:

  • Pending US legal counsel sign-off: all engineering controls for ESIGN, UETA and NY ESRA are built in bridge v2.0.15, and qualified US counsel is reviewing the US-ESIGN-UETA-v3 consent wording, the no-charge paper-copy promise and state exclusions prior to commercial rollout.
  • No PAdES / HSM digital seal yet: not required by ESIGN or UETA for simple contracts; hardware-backed PAdES B-LT sealing with RFC 3161 trusted timestamps is on our Enterprise roadmap.
  • Transparent hosting & privacy: neither ESIGN nor UETA restricts server location; hosting details and sub-processors are disclosed on our Security page to support state privacy disclosures.

ESIGN § 7003 & UETA § 3(b)

What Send & Sign is for — and not for — under US law.

When a sender selects a US governing law in Step 1 of the New Envelope wizard, Send & Sign displays this exclusion list directly inside SharePoint.

Designed for

  • Simple commercial contracts and B2B service agreements
  • Quotes, statements of work and interactive HTML proposals
  • Non-disclosure agreements (NDAs)
  • Standard employment agreements and offer letters
  • Internal and external multi-party approvals (up to 20 signers)

Supported file types: PDFs and interactive HTML proposals only. Word, Excel and PowerPoint files must be saved as PDF first.

Not supported under US law

  • Wills, codicils and testamentary trusts
  • Adoption, divorce and other family-law matters
  • Court orders, notices and official court documents
  • Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) transactions other than sales and leases of goods (Articles 2 & 2A)
  • Notices of utility cancellation, or of default, foreclosure, eviction or repossession on a primary residence
  • Notices cancelling health or life insurance, product recalls, or documents accompanying hazardous materials
  • Anything that must be notarised, recorded or witnessed (such as real-property deeds, powers of attorney or health-care proxies); New York ESRA and individual states add further exclusions
!
