E‑signature compliance for Australia.

Built for simple contracts under the Electronic Transactions Act 1999 (Cth) and the harmonised State and Territory Electronic Transactions Acts.

ETA 1999 (Cth) s 9, s 10 & s 12

How Send & Sign meets each requirement.

Every envelope sent under Australian law follows the same verifiable chain — from identity check through consent, signing and retention.

Statutory or case-law requirement How Send & Sign meets it today
s 10(1)(a) — Identification
Who signed		 Each signer receives a unique 256-bit single-use link bound to their email address and must enter a mandatory 6-digit email code before the document unlocks (link-only signing is refused; five wrong codes lock the check). The signer recorded on the certificate is always the code-verified recipient — any differently typed name is stored separately as an adopted signature name.
s 10(1)(a) — Intention
They meant to sign		 Consent and signing remain locked until the document opens on the signer's device. The signer actively draws, types or uploads a signature, ticks the electronic-signature consent box, and confirms with a deliberate Sign and complete action (or Decline with a reason), recorded as its own IntentConfirmed event.
s 10(1)(b) — Reliability & Williams Group v Crocker [2016] NSWCA 265 Eliminates the shared-password failure mode: mandatory per-recipient 6-digit email OTP, single-use links that cannot be replayed (410 Gone after use), real signer IP address and browser captured per recipient, and server-computed OriginalSha256 verification (any mismatch is refused).
s 10(1)(c) — Consent & paper opt-out
Agreed to sign electronically		 The consent wording (AU-ETA-1999-v1) is defined and served by our signing service — never by the signer's browser — and states that electronic signing is binding and that the signer may choose paper instead. Each signer's acceptance is recorded with the version and SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact wording shown, and printed on the certificate.
s 9 (Writing) & s 12 (Retention & integrity)
Accessible & unchanged		 Computes pre-signing OriginalSha256 and post-signing SignedSha256, chains every event with RFC 8785 canonical JSON + SHA-256, appends a multi-page Certificate of Electronic Signature, attaches the signed PDF (up to 10 MB) to completion emails for every party, and saves {OriginalName}_Signed.pdf (or _Signed.html) back into your SharePoint folder next to the untouched original.

Australian case law, designed in

The right person, provably.

In Williams Group Australia Pty Ltd v Crocker [2016] NSWCA 265, the court would not bind a person to an electronic signature that could not be shown to be theirs.

Send & Sign tethers every signing session to the specific recipient it was issued to with a mandatory 6-digit email code, real client IP and browser capture. The signer's identity on the certificate is always the token-bound, OTP-verified recipient — if someone types a different name, it is recorded separately as an "adopted" signature name.

Evidence on every document

What travels with every signed copy.

  • Multi-page Certificate of Electronic Signature: states the Australian ETA s 10 simple-contract scope (SimpleContract-ETA1999-s10), sender, sent and completed times, identity check used, every signer's OTP verification time, signing time, IP address, browser, adopted name, consent version + text SHA-256, and every event in the signing history.
  • Clear timestamps: certificate and audit trail events show UTC and Perth time (AWST); visual signature stamps display local AWST time.
  • Independent verification: export the full RFC 8785 hash-chained audit trail as a JSON evidence pack, or use one-click Verify HTML integrity for signed interactive proposals.
  • A copy for every party: completion emails attach the signed PDF (up to 10 MB) for the sender and every signer, while your permanent copy is filed in SharePoint.
  • Australian privacy notice: signers see an APP 5 collection notice. Invitation, code and receipt emails are strictly transactional.

Stated plainly

What we don't do yet

We prefer to be clear about what is live today versus what is on our roadmap:

  • No PAdES / HSM digital seal yet: signed PDFs are not yet certificate-sealed, so Adobe Acrobat does not show a "certified" banner. Integrity is demonstrated via SHA-256 fingerprints, the multi-page certificate and the hash-chained audit trail. Hardware-backed PAdES B-LT sealing with RFC 3161 trusted timestamps is on our Enterprise roadmap.
  • Planned for the Enterprise edition, not yet available: sealing with a hardware security module (Azure Key Vault Managed HSM), SMS one-time codes and Digital ID verification (myID / ConnectID).

Data minimisation & retention

Your SharePoint stays the system of record.

Our signing service holds zero SharePoint or Microsoft Graph permissions and deletes documents as soon as they are no longer needed.

Item on the signing service When it is deleted
Original document (once signing completes) As soon as your SharePoint confirms it holds the signed copy (verified against its SHA-256 fingerprint).
Signed copy 7 days after SharePoint save-back confirmation (so signers have a download window in addition to their emailed copy).
Original document (declined, cancelled or expired) Within the hour.
Remaining envelope record (names, emails, fingerprints, audit trail) After 90 days. Fingerprints and your signed copy stay in your SharePoint for as long as you keep them.

Backups and the full list of data are covered in the signing bridge disclosure.

Clear scope

What Send & Sign is for — and not for.

Senders see a clear "simple contracts only" notice in Step 1 of the New Envelope wizard.

Designed for

  • Commercial contracts and service agreements
  • Quotes and interactive HTML proposals
  • Non-disclosure agreements (NDAs)
  • HR agreements and offer letters
  • Internal and external multi-party approvals (up to 20 signers)

Supported file types: PDFs and interactive HTML proposals only. Word, Excel and PowerPoint files must be saved as PDF first.

Not supported under Australian law

  • Deeds, including company deeds executed under Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s 127
  • Documents that must be witnessed
  • Wills, powers of attorney (including enduring) and advance health directives
  • Statutory declarations, affidavits and court documents
  • Land-registry instruments (use PEXA or another ELNO)
  • Some documents have further State and Territory rules: get legal advice if you are unsure
